# Write a parts enquiry from a repair order

Parts Relay workshop for repair shops, about 10 minutes

## On the bench

A repair order with a diagnosed fault. Keep customer names and contact details off the worksheet.

## What you’ll write

A parts enquiry ready to send to your own distributor.

## When a symptom stands in for the part name

A door that won’t stay shut tells the counter about the fault. A damaged right rear latch tells them which item to look up. If the repair also needs an actuator, find out whether it comes with the latch.

## Write up the request

1. Find the technician’s diagnosis
   Underline the component they identified. If you can only find a symptom, ask what they intend to replace before requesting a price.

2. Describe one orderable item
   Record the vehicle from the job card. Name the part with its fitting position and quantity. For a door latch, that means which door, which side and how many.

3. Ask about anything supplied with it
   Turn the unresolved detail into a question the distributor can answer on the quote. If the actuator is needed, ask whether it comes with the latch or carries its own reference.

## Check it with the technician

Read the finished request to the technician. Correct any difference between the part they intend to fit and the item the supplier is about to price.

## Read it as the distributor

Vehicle
Use the verified identification for this job.

Item and quantity
Would someone reading the enquiry know which door the latch goes on and how many to price?

Technician’s finding
Keep the finding that explains the scope of the repair. Additional symptoms belong on the job card unless they affect the choice of part.

Question for the counter
Name the component whose inclusion needs confirming. “Does the latch come with the actuator?” gives the distributor something concrete to check.

## Use it on another job

For a window regulator, record the affected door and ask whether the quoted regulator includes the motor.
