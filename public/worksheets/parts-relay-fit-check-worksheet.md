# An alternator fit check

Parts Relay workshop for distributors, about 15 minutes

## Evidence to hand

An alternator enquiry and the original label, a legible photo or the technician’s reading.

## Callback record

A record of what distinguishes the required unit, or a specific detail to ask the technician for.

## When the suffix is unreadable

Start with the marking the technician can see. The output rating might still be clear even when the end of the manufacturer’s reference has rubbed away. Keep both pieces of information.

## Work through the reference

1. Take the markings in order
   Write the vehicle identifier on the sheet. Record the manufacturer’s part number character by character and put the amp rating on a separate line.

2. Mark the gap
   Note where a character is unreadable. Ask for a closer photo if it would settle the reading. Leave a genuine gap open rather than supplying a likely letter from the catalogue.

3. Ask the supplier what separates the units
   If the partial reference leaves more than one candidate, find out which visible detail would decide between them. Give the technician that question before they go back to the car.

## Before you release the quote

Could a colleague point to the evidence for the quoted reference? When two variants still fit the information on hand, keep the quote open until the supplier can distinguish them.

## What belongs in your callback record

Readable markings
Copy only the characters the workshop can verify. Keep units with the rating.

Unresolved characters
Mark whether the gap is at the start, in the middle or at the end of the reference.

Next check
Write the supplier’s exact question about the label, connector or equipment.

Outcome
Record which reference the supplier confirmed, or who will call back with the missing detail.

## Use it on another job

Use the same sheet for a starter motor. Replace the alternator rating with the markings the supplier needs for that starter application.
